The problem

Doctors spend a large share of every OPD consultation just collecting history — symptoms, past reports, medication — before diagnosis even starts.

Who feels it

Elderly and low-literacy patients struggle to describe symptoms; hospitals drown in paperwork; doctors lose time they could spend treating people.

Our approach

Let a multilingual kiosk take the history first — by voice or touch — so the doctor opens the file already knowing what matters.

Proposed solution

Four modules do the work before the patient reaches the doctor.

Each one is demonstrated in the interactive kiosk walkthrough.

01 · Conversation

Multilingual chatbot

Has a short, guided conversation with the patient, asks follow-up questions about their complaint, and collects the basic information a doctor would otherwise ask for first.

02 · Intake

Document scanner

Scans prescriptions and lab reports, extracts the values and conclusions that matter, and stores them securely against the patient's record.

03 · Intelligence

Summary generator

Turns the conversation and scanned documents into a short clinical summary — a likely list of conditions and a suggested treatment direction for the doctor to review.

04 · Safety

Red flag detection

If the conversation surfaces symptoms that could be serious, Medikiosk skips the queue and alerts the doctor directly, instead of waiting for the summary.

Beyond the kiosk

The same record keeps working after the visit.

Remote vital monitoring

Doctors can check a patient's vitals even when the patient isn't physically at the hospital.

Smart prescription reminders

Patients get a nudge on their phone when it's time to take medication prescribed at the visit.

Geolocal doctor availability

Patients can see which doctors are available nearby before they travel to a hospital.

Impact

Everyone in the OPD gets time back.

Doctor

Opens a structured summary with history and prior reports already organised, and can focus on diagnosis rather than data entry.

Patient

Describes symptoms in their own language, by voice or touch, and spends less time waiting to be understood.

Hospital

Cuts manual documentation and keeps records interoperable, so OPDs manage higher patient volumes without more paperwork.
